Practical guide · Tourism and hospitality

Fill your low season, instead of suffering it every year.

High season fills everything, then come quieter months that weigh on cash flow and morale. The businesses that cope best do not suffer the low season, they prepare it with different customers and different offers.

    01 · The situation

    Why the low season hurts so much

    On many islands, tourism activity is concentrated over a few months. The rest of the year, costs keep running, staff must be paid and investments wait. Many businesses respond by cutting prices on platforms, which reduces their margin even more.

    Yet other customers exist outside the peak: island residents, the diaspora coming home for school holidays, Carnival or family events, local companies organising team events, associations and schools.

    02 · The levers

    Five practical levers for the low season

    1. Island residents

    Weekend offers, lunch menus, family activities. They are there all year and recommend you to others.

    2. The diaspora

    School holidays, festivals, family visits. Targeted communication to the diaspora in the UK, the US or Canada can fill quiet periods.

    3. Groups and companies

    Team events, conferences, sports clubs, associations. A clear group offer on your website attracts these requests.

    4. Events

    Themed evenings, workshops, private hire. They create a reason to come in a quiet month.

    5. Planned cash flow

    Set aside during the high season what will carry you through the low season, and plan works and holidays then.

    03 · Mistakes to avoid

    What makes the low season worse

    Relying only on discounts

    Cutting prices without changing your audience mostly cuts your margin.

    Communicating too late

    Low season offers need to be prepared and shared weeks ahead.

    Ignoring your data

    Looking at last year's quiet months helps you anticipate.

    Your questions

    Is the low season the same everywhere?

    No. It varies by island, by type of guest and by business. That is why we always start from your own figures.
